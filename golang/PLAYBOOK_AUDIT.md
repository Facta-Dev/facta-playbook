# PLAYBOOK_AUDIT.md — Audit, certification & compliance playbook

Operating contract for how `<app>` prepares for, passes and sustains audits:
**SOC 2, ISO/IEC 27001, ISO 9001** and the other software-relevant
certifications and regulations. It consumes the other playbooks as *control
implementations* and *evidence sources*:

| Playbook | Role in audits |
|---|---|
| `PLAYBOOK_URLS.md` | Permission catalog and RBAC model → logical-access evidence |
| `PLAYBOOK_BACKEND.md` | Architecture, slice isolation, ownership checks, job idempotency, DR design → security, availability, processing-integrity evidence |
| `PLAYBOOK_TESTS.md` | Automated tests, scans and release bundles → the main continuous evidence stream |
| **This file** | Governance, risk, policies, control library, audit lifecycle, vendors, privacy, certifications roadmap |

The rules that carry the whole file:

1. **Test once, comply many.** One internal control library, mapped to every
   framework. We never run a separate compliance effort per certificate.
2. **Compliance as code.** Policies, controls, risks, vendors and evidence
   manifests live in the repository, versioned, reviewed by pull request.
3. **Evidence is a by-product of work**, generated by systems (CI, IdP, cloud,
   ticketing), not assembled by hand before an audit.
4. **Always audit-ready.** Control health is monitored continuously; an audit
   is a sample of what already runs, not a project.
5. **Say what we do, do what we say, prove it.** An undocumented practice or
   an unpracticed document are both findings.
6. **Scope honestly.** Claims, reports and certificates describe the real
   system boundary and nothing broader.

> Scope note: this is an engineering and operations playbook, not legal
> advice or an audit opinion. Control mappings are a working starting point;
> validate them with your auditor, certification body and counsel. Standards
> and regulatory dates change: re-verify editions and deadlines at the start
> of each engagement (status as of November 2026 is noted below).

## 1. Framework landscape and roadmap

### 1.1 What each framework is

| Framework | Nature | Output | Cycle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SOC 2** (AICPA Trust Services Criteria) | Attestation by a licensed CPA firm against criteria you choose: Security (mandatory), Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, Privacy | Report (not a certificate). **Type I** = design at a date; **Type II** = design + operating effectiveness over a period (commonly 3–12 months) | Annual Type II |
| **ISO/IEC 27001:2022** | Certifiable Information Security Management System (ISMS) + Annex A (93 controls in 4 themes: organizational, people, physical, technological) | Certificate from an accredited certification body | 3-year cycle: Stage 1 + Stage 2, annual surveillance, recertification |
| **ISO 9001** | Certifiable Quality Management System (QMS) | Certificate from an accredited certification body | 3-year cycle, as above |
| **Other** | See section 14 | Report, certificate, self-assessment or legal obligation | Varies |

### 1.2 Status notes (verify before relying on them)

- **ISO 27001**: certify against the **2022** edition (the transition from the
  2013 edition has ended). Include the 2024 amendment on climate-change
  considerations in context analysis.
- **ISO 9001**: the revised **ISO 9001:2026** was published on 16 September
  2026; a three-year transition runs to about 30 September 2029, and
  certification bodies must first be accredited for the new edition. Build the
  QMS to the 2026 text; confirm clause numbering and exact requirements
  against the published standard (the revision is evolutionary: process
  approach, PDCA and risk-based thinking stay, with explicit emphasis on
  quality culture and ethical behavior and a clearer separation of
  opportunities from risks).
- **EU Cyber Resilience Act**: manufacturer reporting duties (actively
  exploited vulnerabilities, severe incidents) apply from **11 September
  2026**; most other obligations from 11 December 2027. Assess whether the
  distributed Go binary is a "product with digital elements"; a hosted SaaS
  instance is treated differently. Get counsel's view.

### 1.3 Recommended roadmap for `<app>`

Overlap between SOC 2 and ISO 27001 is high, so they share one control
library and one evidence stream.

| Phase | Window (indicative) | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — Foundation** | Months 0–2 | Scope, governance, policy set, risk assessment, control library, evidence collectors, vendor register |
| **1 — Implement and observe** | Months 2–6 | Controls running, evidence flowing, internal audit #1, management review #1, pen test, readiness assessment |
| **2 — First assurance** | Months 6–9 | SOC 2 Type I (optional stepping stone) and ISO 27001 Stage 1 → Stage 2; start of the SOC 2 Type II observation window |
| **3 — Sustain** | Months 9–15 | SOC 2 Type II report; ISO 27001 certificate; ISO 9001 (2026 edition) gap closure |
| **4 — Expand** | Month 12+ | ISO 9001 certification, ISO 27701 / privacy program, plus market-driven items (section 14) |

SOC 2 categories for the first cycle: **Security + Availability +
Confidentiality**. Add **Processing Integrity** (outbox correctness and
idempotency are a differentiator) and **Privacy** when customers require them.

## 2. Scope and system description

Maintained in `compliance/scope/` and reviewed at least annually and on any
architecture change.

**Deployment model first.** `<app>` ships as software the customer runs: a
static Go binary plus docker-compose, with Postgres in the customer's network.
For a self-hosted deployment `<app>`-the-vendor is not in the data path at
all, so the audit scope is the **software product and its supply chain**
(secure development, build integrity, vulnerability handling, release
evidence, support tooling), not a service desk over customer data. If
`<app>` also runs a hosted instance, that instance is a second, separate
scope with the service-provider controls (availability, backups, incident
response over customer data); do not blur the two in one system description.

- **In-scope system**: backend slices (`PLAYBOOK_BACKEND.md`), the entrypoints
  under `cmd/`, CI/CD, build and release artifacts, the dependency graph,
  documentation. For a hosted instance add: infrastructure, Postgres,
  observability, support tooling, corporate IT (identity provider, endpoints,
  ticketing, code host).
- **Boundary and data flows**: diagrams of trust boundaries, the
  single-`public`-schema data model with table prefixes (`PLAYBOOK_BACKEND.md`
  §3), external connections (SMTP, OAuth providers), and data stores by
  classification.
- **Data classification**: Public / Internal / Confidential / Restricted.
  Restricted covers credentials and provider keys, session and refresh-token
  metadata, password hashes, audit-log records, and personal data in user
  profiles.
- **Subservice organizations**: code host, CI runners, artifact registry, SMTP
  provider, OAuth providers. A self-hosted deployment adds the customer as the
  operator of the runtime environment — that boundary and its CUECs belong in
  the contract, not in our evidence.
- **Exclusions** are written down with a reason. Anything claimed in marketing
  or a questionnaire must be inside the scope statement.

### 3.2 Documentation hierarchy

```text
Policy (what and why, exec-approved, annual review)
  → Standard (mandatory technical/organizational requirements)
    → Procedure / runbook (how, step by step)
      → Record / evidence (proof it happened)
```

Everything except raw evidence is Markdown in the repo
(`compliance/policies`, `standards`, `procedures`), changed by PR, approved by
the named approver, with version, owner and next-review date in front matter.

## 5. Risk management

- **Methodology** (`compliance/risk/methodology.md`): likelihood × impact on a
  defined scale, inherent and residual score, risk appetite and acceptance
  thresholds approved by the executive sponsor.
- **Risk register** (`compliance/risk/register.yaml`): ID, description, asset
  or process, threat, vulnerability, owner, inherent score, controls applied
  (control IDs), residual score, treatment (mitigate / transfer / accept /
  avoid), due date, status.
- **Triggers beyond the annual review**: new slice, new vendor, new provider
  integration, new data category, major incident, significant architecture
  change, regulatory change. A new slice or externally reachable route
  requires a lightweight **threat model** in its PR.
- **Statement of Applicability**: generated from the control library and
  register (every Annex A control marked applicable / not applicable with
  justification and implementation status).
- **Top risks to track for `<app>`**: an authorization regression that lets
  one user read another user's profile, sessions or widgets (ownership
  isolation); leak of session tokens, refresh-token rows or OAuth secrets;
  duplicate or lost email delivery from the outbox; a compromised dependency
  in the Go module graph; a stale generated artifact (`sqlc`/OpenAPI) silently
  diverging from the schema; insider access on a hosted instance.

## 7. Compliance as code

```text
compliance/
  scope/                 # system description, data-flow diagrams, inventory, classification
  policies/  standards/  procedures/
  controls/              # <APP>-*.yaml control library
  frameworks/            # soc2.yaml, iso27001.yaml, iso9001.yaml, gdpr.yaml ...
                         #   requirement/criterion → control IDs
  risk/                  # methodology, register, treatment plans
  soa/                   # generated Statement of Applicability
  vendors/               # register + assessments + DPAs index
  audits/                # per cycle: plan, PBC list, populations, findings, reports
  capa/                  # corrective and preventive actions
  training/              # curricula, completion exports index
  incidents/             # index of post-mortems (details in incident tool)
  reports/               # generated dashboards and traceability matrices
tools/compliance/        # collectors, validators, report generators
```

Automation:

- **Validators** (run in CI): every control has owner, frequency, evidence
  source and at least one mapping; every framework requirement maps to a
  control or an explicit "not applicable"; links to tests, policies and
  procedures resolve.
- **Collectors** pull evidence from systems of record (identity provider,
  cloud, code host, CI, ticketing, HRIS, device management, vulnerability
  tools) on the control's frequency into the evidence store.
- **Continuous control monitoring**: a dashboard of control status
  (`passing / failing / stale / no-evidence`), failing controls open a ticket
  to the owner automatically.
- A commercial GRC platform can replace parts of this tooling; keep the
  control library in a portable format either way.

## 8. Evidence management

Builds on section 9 of `PLAYBOOK_TESTS.md`.

- **Prefer system-generated evidence** (exports, logs, pipeline records) over
  screenshots and hand-written attestations.
- **Evidence store**: append-only / write-once, hashed manifests, restricted
  and logged access, retention at least as long as the longest audit window
  plus margin (policy-defined), no secrets and no personal data (redact or
  reference).
- **Naming**: `evidence/{control-id}/{period}/{artifact}`, with a manifest
  carrying source, query or command used, collection timestamp (system
  clock), collector version and hash.
- **Populations**: for sampled controls (changes, new hires, leavers,
  incidents, access requests, vendors), keep a reproducible query that
  produces the **complete population** from the system of record, so the
  auditor can test completeness and accuracy of reports we produce.
- **Freshness**: evidence older than its control's frequency plus tolerance
  marks the control `stale` on the dashboard.
- **Manual evidence** (training sign-off, tabletop minutes, management review
  minutes, pen-test report) is stored as dated, signed documents linked to a
  ticket.
- **Chain of custody**: who collected, when, from where, unchanged since.

## 9. Audit lifecycle

### 9.1 SOC 2

1. **Readiness assessment** (internal or by the audit firm): gap list against
   chosen criteria.
2. **Remediate** gaps; freeze the control set for the period.
3. **Type I** (optional): design effectiveness at a date.
4. **Type II observation window**: controls must operate for the whole period;
   a control that fails is recorded, remediated and explained, not hidden.
5. **Fieldwork**: auditor requests (PBC list), walkthroughs, sampling,
   inquiry, observation, re-performance.
6. **Draft report** review (factual accuracy of the system description,
   management responses to exceptions) and **final report**.
7. **Bridge letter** to cover the gap between period end and the customer's
   date when requested.
8. **Annual repeat**; changes to scope or controls during the year are
   documented as they happen.

### 9.2 ISO 27001 and ISO 9001

1. Select an **accredited** certification body (accreditation by a body that
   is signatory of the relevant IAF multilateral arrangement). For ISO
   9001:2026 confirm the body is accredited for the new edition.
2. **Stage 1**: documentation and readiness review.
3. **Stage 2**: implementation and effectiveness audit; nonconformities must
   be corrected (major before certification; minor with an agreed plan).
4. **Certificate** (3 years), **annual surveillance audits**, recertification
   audit in year 3. Internal audit and management review must have happened
   **before** each external audit.
5. Combine ISO 27001 and ISO 9001 (and later 27701) as an **integrated
   management system** with the same certification body to reduce audit days.

### 9.3 Internal audit program

- Annual plan, risk-based, covering every control domain and every clause of
  the certified standards at least once per cycle; higher-risk areas more
  often.
- Auditors are independent of the area audited and competent in the standard
  (internal staff, a peer from another slice, or an external service).
- Method: review documents, sample records from complete populations,
  interview owners, re-perform tests, check against policy and framework
  requirements.
- Findings: **major nonconformity**, **minor nonconformity**, **observation**,
  **opportunity for improvement**. Each has owner, due date and follow-up.
- Output: audit report, findings in `compliance/capa/`, summary to management
  review.

### 9.4 Management review

At least annually, preferably semi-annually. Agenda (covers ISO 27001 and
ISO 9001 inputs):

- Status of actions from previous reviews and changes in context and
  interested parties' requirements.
- Control and KPI performance, audit results (internal, external, customer),
  nonconformities and CAPA status.
- Incidents, risk assessment results and treatment plan status.
- Customer feedback, complaints and satisfaction; quality objectives.
- Vendor performance and changes.
- Resource needs and opportunities for improvement.
- **Outputs**: decisions, assigned actions, resource commitments, signed
  minutes stored as evidence.

### 9.5 Nonconformity and CAPA

`detect → contain → analyze root cause → correct → verify effectiveness →
close → trend`. CAPA records live in `compliance/capa/` with severity,
owner, due date (for example critical ≤ 30 days; set in policy), evidence of
verification and recurrence check. Incidents, failed controls, failed tests in
`PLAYBOOK_TESTS.md`, audit findings and customer complaints all feed the same
CAPA process.

## 11. Quality management (ISO 9001) for a software product

ISO 9001 is process- and customer-focused, so it complements the security
frameworks. Map the QMS onto what already exists:

| QMS element | `<app>` implementation |
|---|---|
| Context, interested parties, scope (4) | `compliance/scope/`, customers, regulators, partners, vendors |
| Leadership, quality policy, roles (5) | Policy set, RACI, management review; quality culture and ethical behavior as an explicit leadership duty (2026 edition) |
| Risks and opportunities, objectives (6) | Risk register + opportunity register; quality objectives with owners |
| Resources, competence, awareness, documented information (7) | Training records, onboarding, `compliance/` repository as controlled documents |
| Customer requirements and communication (8.2) | Product requirements process, SLAs, trust center, change notices |
| **Design and development (8.3)** | `PLAYBOOK_BACKEND.md` (architecture), ADRs, design reviews, threat models, planned verification (`PLAYBOOK_TESTS.md`), validation with customers/beta |
| Externally provided processes (8.4) | Vendor management (section 12), open-source dependency policy |
| Production and service provision (8.5) | Release and deployment procedure, controlled changes, operations runbooks, support |
| Release of products and services (8.6) | Release gates and the release evidence bundle |
| Nonconforming outputs (8.7) | Defect and incident management, rollback procedure, CAPA |
| Monitoring, measurement, customer satisfaction (9.1) | KPIs below, CSAT/NPS, SLO reports |
| Internal audit, management review (9.2, 9.3) | Sections 9.3 and 9.4 |
| Improvement, CAPA (10) | Section 9.5, retrospectives |

Suggested quality KPIs (reviewed in management review): defect escape rate,
change failure rate, mean time to restore, SLO attainment per slice, outbox
drain time, flaky-test rate, customer-reported incidents per release, support
first-response and resolution times, CSAT/NPS, audit findings per cycle,
CAPA on-time closure.

Software-specific frameworks that support (not replace) the QMS: ISO/IEC
25010 product quality model for non-functional requirements; ISO/IEC/IEEE
12207 lifecycle processes as a reference; OWASP ASVS and SAMM for application
security maturity (see section 14).

## 12. Third-party (vendor) risk

- **Register** (`compliance/vendors/register.yaml`): vendor, service, data
  processed and classification, tier, owner, contract and DPA link,
  certifications and reports, sub-processors, region, last review, exit plan.
- **Tiering**: *Critical* (code host, CI runners, artifact registry, and any
  email/OAuth provider enabled by default), *Important*, *Low*. Tier sets
  due-diligence depth and review frequency.
- **Due diligence before onboarding**: SOC 2 report or ISO certificate
  (check scope, period, exceptions and CUECs), security questionnaire,
  pen-test summary, data-processing agreement, breach-notification terms,
  sub-processor list, data location and transfer mechanism, financial and
  continuity viability.
- **Ongoing**: annual review of reports and certificates, monitoring of
  incidents and changes in terms, re-assessment on scope change.
- **Contractual**: security requirements, audit rights where justified,
  incident notification time, return/deletion of data on exit.
- **Technical containment**: provider access through a slice port/adapter
  with least privilege (`PLAYBOOK_BACKEND.md` §2); OAuth client secrets and
  SMTP credentials are configured by the operator and never returned by the
  API (`PLAYBOOK_SECURITY.md` §8); any provider can be swapped behind its port
  without touching a use case. `<APP>_OFFLINE=true` must keep the binary fully
  functional with no provider calls, which is also the evidence that a
  provider is optional.
- **Customer visibility**: published sub-processor list with change
  notification.

## 13. Privacy and product-compliance for `<app>`

`<app>` is a user-facing backend: it stores account data (email, name,
password hash), session metadata, user-generated records and an audit log.

- **Roles**: for a self-hosted deployment the **operator is the controller**
  and `<app>`-the-vendor processes nothing; `<app>` is controller of its own
  website, license, support and billing data only. For a hosted instance the
  operator is the controller of the workspace and `<app>` is a processor under
  a DPA. Write the role per deployment model, not per feature.
- **Records of processing** and a **DPIA** for account data, session metadata
  and the audit log, because each can contain personal data.
- **Retention**: expired sessions and verification/reset codes are purged by
  the janitor; user records and audit logs follow the operator's retention
  policy; deletion cascades are documented and tested.
- **Data-subject rights**: search, export, rectify and erase a subject's data
  across relational tables, the audit log and backups. Deletion is a
  documented cascade with evidence, not a row delete.
- **International transfers**: mechanisms and transfer assessments for each
  sub-processor and region; a self-hosted deployment keeps the data in the
  customer's own region and needs no transfer mechanism of ours.
- **AI features**: if any are added, inventory the models and providers,
  require explicit enablement per provider, no training on customer data,
  prompt/response logging rules, human oversight for any decision affecting a
  person, and evaluation against the EU AI Act and ISO/IEC 42001 as
  applicable. `<APP>_OFFLINE=true` must remain a complete product experience.
- **Breach handling**: privacy incidents follow the incident process with
  regulator and customer notification clocks.

## 15. Customer-facing assurance

- **Trust center** with: current SOC 2 report (under NDA), ISO certificates
  and scope, pen-test executive summary, sub-processor list, DPA template,
  security overview, uptime/status page, policies summary.
- **Questionnaire library**: maintained answers (SIG, CAIQ, custom) linked to
  control IDs; answers are reviewed by the security lead, versioned and never
  contradict the scope statement.
- **Vulnerability disclosure policy** and `security.txt`; triage SLA;
  optional bug-bounty program later.
- **Marketing claims review**: nothing says "certified", "compliant" or
  "SOC 2 compliant" beyond what the reports and certificates support; "SOC 2
  Type II report" is the accurate phrasing.
- **Customer audit rights**: standard process for customer-requested audits
  and questionnaires, relying on third-party reports where possible.

## 16. Audit-day playbook

- **Preparation**: confirm scope and period, assemble populations, check
  dashboard for stale or failing controls, brief control owners, freeze
  unnecessary changes to evidence systems.
- **Auditor access**: read-only, time-boxed, named accounts, logged, to the
  evidence store and systems of record; no shared credentials.
- **Requests (PBC)**: a single tracker, one coordinator, SLA to respond,
  every response references the control ID and evidence path.
- **Walkthroughs**: control owners explain and demonstrate real operation
  (for example, show the pull request, checks and release bundle for a recent
  change).
- **Sampling**: expect samples from every population (changes, joiners,
  leavers, incidents, access reviews, vendors, vulnerabilities). Gaps in a
  population are disclosed, not patched retroactively.
- **Exceptions**: acknowledge, explain root cause and remediation, link the
  CAPA. Never alter evidence or backdate records.
- **After**: management responses, CAPA for findings, lessons learned feed the
  next management review.

## 19. Concrete example: quarterly access review (`<APP>-ACC-03`)

1. **Trigger**: scheduler creates a ticket on the first business day of the
   quarter, assigned to the system owners.
2. **Collection**: collector exports roles from the identity provider, code
   host, and (for a hosted instance) production datastores and the admin
   console (`/api/v1/admin/users` as defined in `PLAYBOOK_URLS.md`) into
   `evidence/<APP>-ACC-03/{quarter}/`.
3. **Comparison**: the export is diffed against the backend role hierarchy in
   `internal/shared/rolex`; unexpected grants are flagged.
4. **Review**: each owner approves or marks removals; removals are executed
   and ticketed; the sign-off JSON records reviewer, time and decisions.
5. **Verification**: the `role-matrix` and `ownership-isolation` suites run
   against staging to prove the authorization model itself still holds.
6. **Evidence**: export, diff, sign-off, removal tickets and test results with
   hashes in the manifest.
7. **Auditor sample**: for the SOC 2 Type II window the auditor selects one or
   more quarters; the population query returns every quarter in the period;
   the ISO auditor sees the same evidence mapped to A.5.18 and A.8.3.
8. **Failure path**: overdue or incomplete review turns the control `failing`
   on the dashboard and opens a CAPA.

## 20. Checklist

- [ ] Scope, boundary, data classification and subservice organizations are
      documented and reviewed annually.
- [ ] Roles, RACI and an executive sponsor are named; every control has a
      named owner.
- [ ] Policy set approved, versioned in the repo and reviewed annually.
- [ ] One control library maps to SOC 2, ISO 27001, ISO 9001 and privacy
      requirements; validators run in CI.
- [ ] Risk methodology, register, treatment plan and Statement of
      Applicability exist and are current.
- [ ] Evidence is system-generated, hashed, append-only, retained per policy
      and free of secrets and personal data.
- [ ] Populations can be reproduced for every sampled control.
- [ ] Continuous control monitoring shows no stale or failing controls, or
      each has a ticket and owner.
- [ ] Internal audit covers all domains and clauses each cycle by independent
      auditors; findings flow into CAPA.
- [ ] Management review held at least annually with minutes and actions.
- [ ] Access reviews, vulnerability SLAs, pen test, DR restore, incident
      tabletop and training run on schedule.
- [ ] Vendor register, tiering, due diligence and annual reviews are current;
      sub-processor list is published.
- [ ] Privacy program: roles, records of processing, DPIAs, data-subject
      rights flows and transfer mechanisms are in place.

      SOC 2 auditor is a licensed CPA firm.
- [ ] Marketing and questionnaire claims match the scope and reports.
- [ ] Applicability of CRA, NIS2/DORA flow-downs, EU AI Act and other
      regulations assessed with counsel and recorded.
